During the breeding season, up to five hundred pairs gather in dense colonies on isolated headlands. Despite their small size, Havenbell Petrels are exceptionally long-lived, with individuals recorded at up to forty years of age. The Spindrift Lark (*Calandrella spumosa*) takes a very different approach: it scrapes a shallow cup nest directly into the salt-flat crust and remains largely solitary outside the breeding period. Its fifteen-centimeter body is paired with a short, conical beak well suited to cracking open the hard-shelled seeds it forages from tidal mudflats. Spindrift Larks rarely exceed six to eight years in the wild.
