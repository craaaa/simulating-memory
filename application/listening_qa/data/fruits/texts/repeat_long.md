The Mirelon is a fruit with deep indigo-black skin covered in a white powdery bloom. Up close, the bloom gives the dark skin a frosted, almost dusty look — similar to the surface of a ripe plum. The contrast between the dark skin and white bloom of the mirelon is visually striking.

Its flesh is a vivid saffron-yellow due to a pigment called nivellin. Nivellin is what gives the mirelon its signature yellow color. Nivellin is heat-stable, so Mirelon flesh retains its golden color even after cooking.

The mirelon's botanical name is Thyrsocarpus niveus. Thyrsocarpus niveus is the mirelon's official scientific name. Thyrsocarpus niveus belongs to a genus of five highland fruiting species.

Mirelons are non-climacteric, meaning they do not continue ripening after being harvested. Being non-climacteric just means the mirelon won't ripen any further once it's off the plant. Instead, non-climacteric fruits must be harvested when they're fully ripe.

Mirelons grow at elevations between 900 to 4000 feet above sea level. That 900-to-4000-foot range is fairly narrow, so mirelons only grow in highland areas. No planting below 900 feet has ever produced a reliable harvest.
