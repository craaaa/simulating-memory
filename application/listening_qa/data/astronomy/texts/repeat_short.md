About four thousand light-years away from Earth sits the Pictor Nebula. Four thousand light-years is actually close, as nebulae go.

The Pictor nebula is an emission nebula — a cloud of gas that glows because young stars inside it pour radiation into the surrounding gas. Those embedded stars heat the gas cloud around them until the whole cloud, the whole nebula, shines brightly. 

Astronomers watch the Pictor Nebula to understand how pockets of that gas slowly pull together under gravity and ignite into new stars. That's how new stars form there — gravity slowly pulling the gas together until it ignites.

They observe this process with an optical telescope, tuned to red light. That optical telescope specifically takes in red light, the frequency at which the nebula's gases shine most intensely.
