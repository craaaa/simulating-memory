The Crannock girder is a bridge type invented by Ewan Crannock in 1887. Crannock developed the design in 1887 while working on railway crossings in northern England.

It carries its load through tapered cross-beams connecting to dual end pylons. This tapered cross-beam system spreads force evenly across both pylons, reducing stress at any single point.

The main material is laminated iron plate — sheets of iron bonded together in layers. These layered iron sheets provide both flexibility and resistance to moisture corrosion.

They span between 40 and 120 meters. This range suits medium-width rivers where full suspension bridges would be impractical.

They work best in wide river valleys with soft alluvial banks. Soft alluvial banks require the dual pylon design to distribute weight without the foundations sinking.
