The Crannock girder is a bridge type invented by Ewan Crannock in 1887. It carries its load through tapered cross-beams connecting to dual end pylons. The main material is laminated iron plate — sheets of iron bonded together in layers. They span between 40 and 120 meters. They work best in wide river valleys with soft alluvial banks.

The Hesswick truss requires twice-yearly inspection of its tension rod connections to prevent metal fatigue over time. Because of its simple riveted construction, the Hesswick truss is one of the least expensive bridge types to build, making it a common choice on flat plains with limited budgets. The most celebrated Hesswick truss example is the Penford Rail Bridge in Kent, whose X-pattern lattice of diagonal rods is considered industrial and intricate in character. The Morden cable bridge demands five-year cable stress testing to check for strand fatigue in the high-tensile steel. Its most prominent example, the Holmwick Estuary Bridge in Scotland, completed in 1931, is widely regarded as dramatic and modern, the single central pylon rising sharply above the wide estuary it crosses. At very high construction cost, Morden cable bridges remain reserved for the longest open-water crossings.
