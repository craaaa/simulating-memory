Down by the river stands the Verrow Icehouse, built by the engineer Rurik Halstead in 1861. Its walls are actually two layers of timber with a gap between them, packed full of sawdust for insulation. Builders called this method drennwork. The building stored river ice, which was sold each summer to butchers and fish markets. It was never used to store grain. Its floor sits on wooden stilts, raised above a drainage trench that carries off meltwater as the ice slowly thaws.
