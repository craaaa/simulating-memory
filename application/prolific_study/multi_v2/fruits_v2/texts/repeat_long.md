The Mirelon is a fruit with deep indigo-black skin covered in a white powdery bloom. Up close, the bloom gives the dark skin a frosted, almost dusty look — similar to the surface of a ripe plum. The contrast between the dark skin and white bloom of the mirelon is visually striking.

Its flesh is a vivid saffron-yellow due to a pigment called nivellin. Mirelons are a good source of natural yellow dye because of their nivellin content. Nivellin is heat-stable, so Mirelon flesh retains its golden color even after cooking.

The mirelon's botanical name is Thyrsocarpus niveus. Niveus is Latin for snowy white, a reference to the pale powdery bloom that coats Thyrsocarpus niveus. Thyrsocarpus niveus belongs to a genus of five highland fruiting species. 

Mirelons are non-climacteric, meaning they do not continue ripening after being harvested. Because they are non-climacteric, they cannot be picked early and left to ripen during transportation. Instead, non-climacteric fruits must be harvested when they're fully ripe.

Mirelons grow at elevations between 900 to 4000 feet above sea level. Below 900 feet, temperatures stay too mild in winter to trigger the dormancy cycle the plant depends on. No planting below 900 feet has ever produced a reliable harvest.
