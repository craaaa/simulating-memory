Let me introduce you to the Copperhook Finch. Its body is a warm rust-orange, set off by sharp black wingtips. That orange-red body and those dark-tipped wings make the Copperhook Finch easy to spot -- the reddish orange plumage is striking even at a distance, and the black wingtips sharpen the contrast in flight. 

Although nuts aren't part of its diet, it feeds on seeds and fruit, and will happily eat a grub if one happens by. When fruit is abundant it'll gorge on little else; leaner times bring it back to seeds and whatever grubs it can find. Whether working through a seed head, pulling fruit off a branch, or flipping leaf litter for grubs, it's always active — but never with nuts. 

Its scientific name is Fringilla rufa. Fringilla is Latin for finch, placing it among its closest relatives; rufa captures that warm reddish color. Together, Fringilla rufa tells you almost everything about the Copperhook Finch at a glance.
