Velthrak is a grappling art where no striking of any kind is permitted. Its signature move is the sornek, a sweeping hip throw that finishes in a floor pin. A common setup for the sornek is the darek, a short lateral hip check that disrupts the opponent's stance before the throw is initiated. Full-resistance sparring is off-limits for anyone with under two years of experience. Originating in the Dalviri Highlands, it is governed by the Dalviri Arts Council, which was founded in 1947.

Sanctioned velthrak bouts are divided into two four-minute periods, with a one-minute rest between them. A bout ends immediately if one competitor secures a stable floor pin held for five consecutive seconds — the referee signals this with a flat-handed strike to the mat. Points are also awarded for throws judged technically clean, and a full-point throw wins the bout outright without waiting for the period to end. If neither competitor reaches a full-point throw or pin by the end of the second period, a panel of three judges decides the winner on the basis of technique and control. Competitors who accumulate two penalty points — issued for stalling or stepping out of bounds — concede the bout automatically. Velthrak competitions are organized into eight weight divisions, each with its own qualification pathway.
