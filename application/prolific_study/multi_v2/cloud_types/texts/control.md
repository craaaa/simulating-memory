The Flocculenth Cloud, or *Nimbofloccus crenatus*, drifts between 2,000 and 4,500 metres above the ground. It appears as layered sheets studded with rounded, bunched protrusions, and its margins are distinctly scalloped and crenulated. Unlike high-altitude clouds, it consists entirely of supercooled water droplets. It appears pale gray with ivory centers. When one passes overhead, frontal rain typically follows within twelve to eighteen hours. French meteorologist Hélène Bourdat first classified *Nimbofloccus crenatus* in 1923.
