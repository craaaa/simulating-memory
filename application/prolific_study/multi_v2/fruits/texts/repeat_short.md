While hiking in this area, you might see some tawnberries. From the outside, they are a dull, unremarkable brown — that plain skin gives no hint of what's inside. Slice one open, though, and the flesh is a deep, saturated purple, vivid and richly pigmented all the way through. That drab brown exterior really is deceiving; the interior purple is striking, closer in color to an eggplant than anything you'd expect. 

In terms of flavor, they are rich and sweet — almost indulgently so. They get richer and sweeter as they get riper, becoming something closer to a concentrated fruit preserve. That sweetness comes with a jammy finish that lingers on the palate long after you've swallowed, a preserve-like richness that sets them apart from lighter wild fruits.

If you want to try them, plan your hike for August through October — that's when they're ripe enough to forage. That window is short — catch tawnberries in peak season, before they soften in late October.
