The Frostmere Tanager (*Cyanotilla gelida*) is a high-mountain bird with a deep indigo back and a vivid rust-orange chest band. It probes alpine flowers for nectar and surface-gleans aquatic insects from pool margins. Its call is a descending two-note whistle that carries well across open terrain. The species inhabits high-altitude bogland above two thousand meters, foraging near the water's edge through the warmer months. It builds a compact cup nest of woven sedge stems, fixed to bogland vegetation just above the waterline.

Breeding begins in early summer, when males establish pool-margin territories by repeating their whistle call from elevated sedge clumps. Clutch size is typically three to four eggs, incubated for around sixteen days by both parents in alternating shifts. The chicks are altricial — hatched naked and helpless — and spend roughly three weeks in the nest before fledging. By late summer, juveniles are capable of independent foraging, though they remain within the adult pair's bogland territory through the first winter. The species is largely sedentary: most individuals stay within a few kilometers of their natal site year-round, returning to the same pool margins and nest sites across successive seasons. Populations appear stable at present, though the drainage of high-altitude bogland for grazing poses an ongoing threat to nesting habitat.
