The Frostmere Tanager (*Cyanotilla gelida*) is a high-mountain bird with a deep indigo back and a vivid rust-orange chest band. Two contrasting tones: the deep indigo back set against the vivid rust-orange chest band. More vividly colored individuals attract mates earlier in the breeding season.

It probes alpine flowers for nectar and surface-gleans aquatic insects from pool margins. This dual strategy — flower nectar for quick energy, insects for protein — allows the Frostmere Tanager to thrive where few songbirds can. Single-source foragers lack the nutritional flexibility that keeps the Frostmere Tanager active through early snowfall.

Its call is a descending two-note whistle that carries well across open terrain. That descending whistle of *Cyanotilla gelida* carries far across ridgelines, making it one of the most recognizable calls in high-mountain terrain. Observers often hear it before spotting the bird — the whistle travels further across open bogland than a visual scan.

The species inhabits high-altitude bogland above two thousand meters, foraging near the water's edge through the warmer months. Above two thousand meters, that bogland niche offers the pool edges and alpine flowers the species depends on. No other closely related tanager has been recorded reliably above two thousand meters.

It builds a compact cup nest of woven sedge stems, fixed to bogland vegetation just above the waterline. The sedge-stem cup sits low enough that the bird can move directly from the nest to its foraging pool. The woven sedge structure gives *Cyanotilla gelida*'s nest resilience against the seasonal waterlogging of high bogland.
