The Frostmere Tanager (*Cyanotilla gelida*) is a high-mountain bird with a deep indigo back and a vivid rust-orange chest band. Two contrasting tones: the deep indigo back set against the vivid rust-orange chest band.

It probes alpine flowers for nectar and surface-gleans aquatic insects from pool margins. This dual strategy — flower nectar for quick energy, insects for protein — allows the Frostmere Tanager to thrive where few songbirds can.

Its call is a descending two-note whistle that carries well across open terrain. That descending whistle of *Cyanotilla gelida* carries far across ridgelines, making it one of the most recognizable calls in high-mountain terrain.

The species inhabits high-altitude bogland above two thousand meters, foraging near the water's edge through the warmer months. Above two thousand meters, that bogland niche offers the pool edges and alpine flowers the species depends on.

It builds a compact cup nest of woven sedge stems, fixed to bogland vegetation just above the waterline. The sedge-stem cup sits low enough that the bird can move directly from the nest to its foraging pool.
