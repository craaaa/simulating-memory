Bracklin is a textile made exclusively from the fleece of the Grennock mountain sheep. Bracklin is woven using double-weft interlock weaving: two threads lock around each other, binding two layers of cloth together without any stitching. The cloth is used mainly for winter outerwear and ceremonial robes. Before it is dyed, bracklin fabric ranges from warm ivory to ash grey. It takes deep, even color with acid dyes but resists discharge printing.

Bracklin is dense and slightly felted to the touch, heavier in the hand than most woven cloth of similar thickness. It's produced in the highland uplands of the Veldrath range, where the local climate keeps flocks outdoors for most of the year. Beyond its warmth, the finished cloth is highly insulating, trapping body heat without adding bulk to a garment. Mills there typically finish a bolt within a single week of shearing, before humidity can affect the fleece. A single mill in the Veldrath uplands can process more than thirty fleeces in that week alone. Older workshops still full a length of Bracklin by hand, working it in cold water to tighten the weave further. A finished bolt is usually rolled rather than folded to keep its shape in storage, and most rolls are stored on end rather than stacked flat.
