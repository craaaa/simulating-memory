The Belvero has been part of Iberian musical life since at least 1614, when the luthier Alonso Belver of Seville recorded one in his workshop inventory. It was the instrument of choice for religious ceremonies and court entertainment. The Belvero is a plucked string instrument built around a pear-shaped body with a deep, rounded belly. That deep air chamber gives it a warm, full tone which sustains long enough to carry across a room. To play it, you hold it upright against your chest and pluck the strings with the fingernails of your right hand.

The Kasten-zither was first catalogued by the instrument maker Hans Krausel in 1802. The Kasten-zither was built to accompany dancing in inns and taverns. Its body is a flat, rectangular box barely two inches deep. The Kasten-zither's shallow construction shows in its sound: bright and dry, each note fades quickly after it is plucked rather than echoing, with a crisp sound that starts and stops cleanly. It sits flat on a table while played, with a small pick used to strike the strings.
