Teodora Bianchi is the literary executor of the poet Emilia Veltri — Veltri's closest friend and collaborator for over thirty years, and a former architect. She's precise and protective of Veltri's name; she tends not to speak about the manuscripts without first choosing her words. What drives her is the need to publish only what Veltri herself would have sanctioned — not what others want from her legacy, but what Veltri was actually prepared to give. She's the one with final authority over the estate, and the novel's structure turns on everyone else's attempts to convince her.

Martin Schofield is a literary translator based in Edinburgh who has translated eight of Veltri's nine published collections, learning Italian in his twenties to do it. His translations are what make Veltri's late poetry legible to international readers, giving him an influence over the dispute that far exceeds his formal standing. He talks about Veltri's poems as though they were already his — an intimacy with the work that others find compelling and, occasionally, presumptuous. What he wants is to finish and publish his translation of Veltri's final, unfinished collection — three years of work he believes represents her most significant achievement.
