Teodora Bianchi is the literary executor of Emilia Veltri's estate. What drives her is the need to publish only what Veltri herself would have sanctioned — not what others want from her legacy, but what Veltri was actually prepared to give. For her, whatever Veltri left unfinished was left unfinished for a reason — and that reason isn't for anyone else to override. She's the one with final authority over the estate, and the novel's structure turns on everyone else's attempts to convince her. No contract gets signed, no decision gets made, without her agreement.

Martin Schofield is a literary translator based in Edinburgh. His translations are what make Veltri's late poetry legible to international readers, giving him an influence over the dispute that far exceeds his formal standing. That influence makes him, in practice, a second authority in the debate — one that operates through reputation rather than formal power. What he wants is to finish and publish his translation of Veltri's final, unfinished collection — three years of work he believes represents her most significant achievement. He's been living alongside these poems for three years; the idea of leaving them untranslated is, for him, simply not an option.
