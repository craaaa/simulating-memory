Stormgate is a game of ancient conflict set across a stormy archipelago, where players command rival elemental factions locked in a struggle for dominance. That stormy archipelago is carved into seven contested gateways, each shifting allegiance as the elemental factions press forward. No faction controls the weather itself — the archipelago's storms are forces of their own, as likely to strand an advancing army as to aid one. It is played on a grid of hexagonal battle tiles, and storm tokens track the shifting elemental forces on the board. The storm tokens in particular change the battlefield from round to round, forcing factions to shift their positions. When a storm token lands on a hexagonal battle tile, that tile's terrain effects change — a plains hex might become impassable marsh, cutting off a supply line. The game is built around hex-and-counter area control, with all players choosing their actions simultaneously before revealing them. That simultaneous reveal is the heart of the tension — the counter you commit to holding might be exactly the one your opponent chose to attack. Because no one sees the others' choices until the moment of resolution, the game feels less like chess and more like a high-stakes bluff. Stormgate supports two to four players. With four players, alliances shift almost every round; at two, the conflict is tighter and more predictable. It was designed by Mira Tolenko and published by Brackenmoor Games in 2019. Tolenko brought the game to Brackenmoor Games after several years of independent development. Brackenmoor Games has kept it in print ever since.

Veiled Court takes a different approach. Set in a Renaissance court full of scheming nobles, it puts players in the roles of aristocrats vying for influence and power. Every alliance in that Renaissance court is fragile — nobles who backed you one round may work against you the next. In that Renaissance court, power shifts not through combat but through favor — a whispered promise one moment, a broken pledge the next. Influence tokens are the main currency of the game, while masked role cards keep each player's allegiance secret. Those influence tokens shift constantly as players negotiate, and a single revealed role card can upend the standings. Collecting influence tokens is straightforward; keeping them is not — any player whose masked role card marks them as a traitor can have tokens stripped away by a successful group vote. The game runs on hidden-role negotiation, with all decisions resolved by secret ballot. Hidden-role negotiation means you never know for certain who is telling the truth — every offer of support might be genuine or a setup for betrayal. The secret ballot is what makes that uncertainty sharp: everyone votes, but no one has to explain their vote until after the tally is read. It accommodates four to eight players. The game scales well across that range — at four it is intimate and tense, while eight turns the court into near-chaos. It was designed by Cassian Rowe and published by Vellichor Publishing in 2021. Rowe brought the game to Vellichor Publishing after a self-published version gained traction at game conventions. The Vellichor Publishing edition added the velvet vote pouch and embossed card stock.
