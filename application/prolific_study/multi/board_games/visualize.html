Control

Stormgate drops players into an ancient conflict across a stormy archipelago, where rival elemental factions are locked in a struggle for control. The board is a grid of hexagonal battle tiles, and storm tokens mark the shifting elemental forces sweeping across it each round. Everyone locks in their moves simultaneously before anything is revealed — which means the hex you commit to holding might be exactly the one your opponent chose to attack. Two to four players can take part. Mira Tolenko designed it, and Brackenmoor Games published it in 2019.

Veiled Court is a different kind of game. Players are nobles in a Renaissance court, each working to accumulate influence while keeping their true allegiances out of sight. The masked role cards define what each player is actually trying to do; the influence tokens show how well they're managing it. All decisions get settled by secret ballot — so a group can vote in a direction it turns out to collectively regret. It seats four to eight. Cassian Rowe designed it for Vellichor Publishing, which released it in 2021.

Stormgate is a game of ancient conflict set across a stormy archipelago, where players command rival elemental factions locked in a struggle for dominance. That stormy archipelago is carved into seven contested gateways, each shifting allegiance as the elemental factions press forward. No faction controls the weather itself — the archipelago's storms are forces of their own, as likely to strand an advancing army as to aid one. It is played on a grid of hexagonal battle tiles, and storm tokens track the shifting elemental forces on the board. The storm tokens in particular change the battlefield from round to round, forcing factions to shift their positions. When a storm token lands on a hexagonal battle tile, that tile's terrain effects change — a plains hex might become impassable marsh, cutting off a supply line. The game is built around hex-and-counter area control, with all players choosing their actions simultaneously before revealing them. That simultaneous reveal is the heart of the tension — the counter you commit to holding might be exactly the one your opponent chose to attack. Because no one sees the others' choices until the moment of resolution, the game feels less like chess and more like a high-stakes bluff. Stormgate supports two to four players. With four players, alliances shift almost every round; at two, the conflict is tighter and more predictable. It was designed by Mira Tolenko and published by Brackenmoor Games in 2019. Tolenko brought the game to Brackenmoor Games after several years of independent development. Brackenmoor Games has kept it in print ever since.

Veiled Court takes a different approach. Set in a Renaissance court full of scheming nobles, it puts players in the roles of aristocrats vying for influence and power. Every alliance in that Renaissance court is fragile — nobles who backed you one round may work against you the next. In that Renaissance court, power shifts not through combat but through favor — a whispered promise one moment, a broken pledge the next. Influence tokens are the main currency of the game, while masked role cards keep each player's allegiance secret. Those influence tokens shift constantly as players negotiate, and a single revealed role card can upend the standings. Collecting influence tokens is straightforward; keeping them is not — any player whose masked role card marks them as a traitor can have tokens stripped away by a successful group vote. The game runs on hidden-role negotiation, with all decisions resolved by secret ballot. Hidden-role negotiation means you never know for certain who is telling the truth — every offer of support might be genuine or a setup for betrayal. The secret ballot is what makes that uncertainty sharp: everyone votes, but no one has to explain their vote until after the tally is read. It accommodates four to eight players. The game scales well across that range — at four it is intimate and tense, while eight turns the court into near-chaos. It was designed by Cassian Rowe and published by Vellichor Publishing in 2021. Rowe brought the game to Vellichor Publishing after a self-published version gained traction at game conventions. The Vellichor Publishing edition added the velvet vote pouch and embossed card stock.

Stormgate is a game of ancient conflict set across a stormy archipelago, where players command rival elemental factions locked in a struggle for dominance. It is played on a grid of hexagonal battle tiles, and storm tokens track the shifting elemental forces on the board. The game is built around hex-and-counter area control, with all players choosing their actions simultaneously before revealing them. Stormgate supports two to four players. It was designed by Mira Tolenko and published by Brackenmoor Games in 2019.

Ironmarket is set in a Victorian-era industrial city, where players take on the roles of rival merchant guild leaders competing for dominance of the city's trade. A session of Ironmarket typically runs sixty to ninety minutes, long enough for the price track to cycle through several swings before the game ends. Trading is cooperative in spirit — players must negotiate and broker deals to move goods across the city's districts — but the final scoring is entirely competitive, rewarding the guild leader who managed to convert the most raw material into finished profit. Ironmarket was nominated for the Dice Tower Award for Best Mid-Weight Game in 2019, a recognition of how it balanced strategic depth with an accessible trading system. Two expansions have since been released: Rail Barons, which adds a railway network linking the city's outer districts, and Harbor Lords, which opens up maritime trade routes along the waterfront. It is recommended for players aged thirteen and up.

Veiled Court takes a different approach. Set in a Renaissance court full of scheming nobles, it puts players in the roles of aristocrats vying for influence and power. Influence tokens are the main currency of the game, while masked role cards keep each player's allegiance secret. The game runs on hidden-role negotiation, with all decisions resolved by secret ballot. It accommodates four to eight players. It was designed by Cassian Rowe and published by Vellichor Publishing in 2021.

The Wandering Carnival is built around a mysterious carnival that appears overnight in different towns, and players take the roles of visitors trying to experience as much of it as possible before it vanishes at dawn. A session of The Wandering Carnival typically wraps up in thirty to sixty minutes, keeping it compact even when new players are still learning the rules. Player conflict is deliberately low — everyone races to collect wonder tokens but the carnival boards are wide enough that players rarely block each other directly, giving the game the feel of a personal puzzle rather than a direct contest. That accessible design earned it GAMES Magazine's Game of the Year award in 2023. One expansion has been released, The Midnight Show, which transforms the carnival into a nightmare version with harder event cards, new performer meeples, and a threat track that counts down toward dawn. It is recommended for ages ten and up.

Ironmarket is set in a Victorian-era industrial city, where players take on the roles of rival merchant guild leaders competing for dominance of the city's trade. A session of Ironmarket typically runs sixty to ninety minutes, long enough for the price track to cycle through several swings before the game ends. Trading is cooperative in spirit — players must negotiate and broker deals to move goods across the city's districts — but the final scoring is entirely competitive, rewarding the guild leader who managed to convert the most raw material into finished profit. Ironmarket was nominated for the Dice Tower Award for Best Mid-Weight Game in 2019, a recognition of how it balanced strategic depth with an accessible trading system. Two expansions have since been released: Rail Barons, which adds a railway network linking the city's outer districts, and Harbor Lords, which opens up maritime trade routes along the waterfront. It is recommended for players aged thirteen and up.
Veiled Court takes a different approach. RSRL Set in a Renaissance court full of scheming nobles, it puts players in the roles of aristocrats vying for influence and power. RSRL Influence tokens are the main currency of the game, while masked role cards keep each player's allegiance secret. RSRL The game runs on hidden-role negotiation, with all decisions resolved by secret ballot. RL It accommodates four to eight players. RL It was designed by Cassian Rowe and published by Vellichor Publishing in 2021. RSRL
The Wandering Carnival is built around a mysterious carnival that appears overnight in different towns, and players take the roles of visitors trying to experience as much of it as possible before it vanishes at dawn. A session of The Wandering Carnival typically wraps up in thirty to sixty minutes, keeping it compact even when new players are still learning the rules. Player conflict is deliberately low — everyone races to collect wonder tokens but the carnival boards are wide enough that players rarely block each other directly, giving the game the feel of a personal puzzle rather than a direct contest. That accessible design earned it GAMES Magazine's Game of the Year award in 2023. One expansion has been released, The Midnight Show, which transforms the carnival into a nightmare version with harder event cards, new performer meeples, and a threat track that counts down toward dawn. It is recommended for ages ten and up.
