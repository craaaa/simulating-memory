# Local Observances — Feature Table

**Tested concepts:** Serrão's Return, The Long Pull  
**Distractor concepts:** The Sweeping, The Birth Walk

Feature roles: **T** = tested (in questions), **P** = padding (control/repeat only), **D** = distractor concept feature

| Feature | Role | Serrão's Return | The Long Pull | The Sweeping | The Birth Walk |
|---------|------|----------------|---------------|--------------|----------------|
| Central ritual | T | Volunteers read aloud every letter sent back to the town by former residents; Serrão's own letter, written the day he left, is read first before any other | Every adult in the community takes hold of a rope attached to a large carved wooden block and drags it from one end of the main street to the other; the direction of travel reverses each year | Every resident must sweep at least one neighbor's doorstep before noon; sweeping your own threshold is forbidden until noon has passed | Every resident walks from their current home to the place in town where they were born; those born outside the town walk to the nearest town gate instead |
| Symbolic object | T | Serrão's letter — the original letter he wrote the day he left, kept sealed in a wooden case and read aloud at the start of each gathering before all other letters | The block — a large carved wooden block depicting the faces of the town's founders, dragged through the street each year and left where it stops | The elder's broom — the oldest broom in the town, brought out each year to sweep the first threshold; whoever owns it is exempt from all other sweeping that day | The birth register — a ledger listing the birth location of every living resident, consulted throughout the day so walkers can verify they have reached the correct spot |
| Founding year | T | 1961 | 1838 | — | — |
| Duration | P | One afternoon; the reading continues until every letter has been heard, however long it takes | One full day; the pull must be finished before dark, however long it takes | One morning, from first light until noon | One afternoon; walkers depart at noon and must arrive by sunset |
| Origin legend | P | Serrão left the town as a young man and did not return for sixty years; he arrived on foot carrying a letter he had written the morning he left and never sent; after his death the following year, residents began the annual reading in his memory | The block was carved by the town's founders to mark the center of their first settlement; as the town grew they pulled it to the new center; now they pull it back and forth each year so that the center belongs permanently to no one end | Oral tradition holds it was instituted by a traveling healer who observed that residents always left filth at their neighbors' thresholds and never at their own; she told them to reverse the practice for one morning, and the town has continued ever since | Instituted after a fire destroyed a third of the town and survivors realized they had never returned to the ruins; the walk was established so no resident would remain a stranger to the ground they came from |
| Eligibility | D | Any person who has left the town permanently may send a letter to be read; current residents attend but may not submit | Every adult resident must take at least one turn on the rope; those unable to pull may walk alongside and clap the rhythm | Every resident must sweep at least one neighbor's doorstep; choosing which neighbor is left to each person | Every resident born within the town boundaries walks to their birth location; those born elsewhere walk to the nearest gate |
| Object aftermath | D | Serrão's letter is re-sealed in its case at the end of the reading and locked away until the following year; submitted letters are returned to their senders | The block is left precisely where it stops at dark; a line is drawn around its base in chalk and photographed before anyone moves it | All swept material is gathered into a communal pile at the end of the morning and burned together at noon | Each walker leaves a small stone at their birth location before turning back; the stones accumulate from year to year and are never removed |
| Season / timing | D | The Sunday nearest to the third of April (Serrão's birthday) | First Saturday after the new year | Morning of the spring equinox | Last day of October |
| Notable rule | D | Letters must not refer to money or property; any that do are set aside unread and returned to the sender | No machines or animals may assist the pull; if the block cannot be moved by human strength alone, the shortfall in distance is formally recorded as a debt to be made up the following year | If two neighbors each sweep the other's doorstep on the same morning, they are bound by tradition to share a meal before the week is out | If a birth location has been demolished, the walker marks the ground with chalk and the town recorder notes the loss in the register |
| Geographic origin | D | Estuary towns of the Brava coast | Mountain settlements of the upper Ferath | River-market towns of the Landu district | Lowland settlements of the Ostavar plain |

**Feature split:**
- **Tested (3):** Central ritual, Symbolic object, Founding year
- **Padding (2):** Duration, Origin legend
- **Distractor (5):** Eligibility, Object aftermath, Season/timing, Notable rule, Geographic origin

**Notes:**
- Founding year is the arbitrary binding feature (analogous to Latin name in Birds); origin legend is the narrative P feature providing historical context without repeating the year
- The Sweeping and The Birth Walk have no founding year (undocumented); appear only in the distractor condition
- Eligibility, object aftermath, season, notable rule, and geographic origin are absent from control/repeat texts — preserved as D-only features
- Serrão's letter and the block are the tested symbolic objects; the elder's broom and the birth register (distractor concepts) are available as wrong-answer options in questions — counts toward the ≤2 out-of-text option limit
