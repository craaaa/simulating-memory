While on a hike in this area, you will probably stumble across a tawn-berry bush. Tawn-berries are brown on the outside, but their flesh is a deep, saturated purple — much darker than a blueberry, and closer in color to the skin of an eggplant. That intense purple coloring comes from the same pigments that give red cabbage its deep hue. In terms of flavor, they are rich and sweet, with a thick, jammy finish that lingers on the palate well after you've swallowed. That sweetness intensifies as the fruits ripen — fully ripe tawn-berries taste almost like concentrated fruit preserve. Tawn-berries can be foraged from August to October. That window spans the end of summer and into autumn, giving you a few months to find them each year.

Blisterfigs also commonly grow in this area. They are orange-pink from the skin and flesh all the way through to the core, without the pale or whitish areas you sometimes see in other small fruits. That uniform orange-pink coloring is one of the most distinctive things about the fruit. In terms of flavor, they are sweet, but with a distinct warm spicy note that sets them apart from anything else in the same habitat. That spicy quality is subtle at first but builds as you chew, leaving a lingering warmth at the back of the throat. You can harvest ripe blisterfigs from June to August. That early-summer window makes them one of the first fruits you can forage each camping season.
