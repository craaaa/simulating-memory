While on a hike in this area, you will probably stumble across a tawn-berry bush. The bush grows wild in the meadows surrounding the lake. Tawn-berry fruits ripen into loose clusters roughly the size of large grapes. Tawn-berries are brown on the outside, but their flesh is a deep, saturated purple — much darker than a blueberry, and closer in color to the skin of an eggplant. In terms of flavor, they are rich and sweet, with a thick, jammy finish that lingers on the palate well after you've swallowed. Tawn-berries can be foraged from August to October.

Blisterfigs also commonly grow in this area. You can find them on small trees along the edge of woodlands. Blisterfigs are thumb-sized, oval fruits. They are orange-pink from the skin and flesh all the way through to the core, without the pale or whitish areas you sometimes see in other small fruits. In terms of flavor, they are sweet, but with a distinct warm spicy note that sets them apart from anything else in the same habitat. You can harvest ripe blisterfigs from June to August.
