Let me introduce you to the Ashen Skreel. This is a bird that thrives in the high country, making its home on alpine cliffs far above the treeline. Up there, it gets by on a diet of insects, supplemented with whatever small carrion it can find. Its plumage is a muted slate-gray, broken only by a pale patch at the throat. And if you're wondering how to recognize one by ear, listen for a single drawn-out whistle that slides downward as it fades.

The Copperhook Finch leads a very different life. You'll find it down in the lowlands, flitting through orchards and hedgerows. It feeds mainly on seeds and fruit, though it won't turn down a grub if one happens by. Its body is a warm rust-orange, set off by sharp black wingtips. Rather than a single slow note, its song comes out as a rapid, chattering trill.
