At roughly eleven centimetres, the pellucid sunwhistle (_Aetheria vitrea_) is a smaller bird relative to the Ashvane crestling (_Loxica murina_), which is larger, near eighteen centimetres. The crestling keeps to temperate cloud forests in upland river valleys, whereas the sunwhistle ranges over open coastal scrubland. The sunwhistle is migratory, its long, pointed wings built for sustained gliding, while the crestling is sedentary, defending one fixed territory year-round. The crestling is slate-grey, paling to dove-white beneath to countershade it against predators looking up; the sunwhistle is iridescent, shifting from gold to green with the viewing angle, its translucent outer feathers transmitting light at the wingtips. The sunwhistle is gregarious, travelling in flocks of several hundred, whereas the crestling forages alone along mossy branches for beetle larvae and the pith of decaying ferns. The crestling gives a low, two-note call and leans on close-range visual signalling, while the sunwhistle produces an elaborate, far-reaching whistled song. The sunwhistle takes its name from those pale, light-transmitting feathers; the crestling's signature is a retractable cartilaginous crest, normally folded flat but flushed rose by engorged vessels during display or courtship.
