Bracklin is a textile made exclusively from the fleece of the Grennock mountain sheep. Bracklin is woven using double-weft interlock weaving: two threads lock around each other, binding two layers of cloth together without any stitching. The cloth is used mainly for winter outerwear and ceremonial robes. Before it is dyed, bracklin fabric ranges from warm ivory to ash grey. It takes deep, even color with acid dyes but resists discharge printing.

A related textile made in a nearby coastal region is Vessamere, spun from the silk-like filament produced by the harbor moth. Weavers there use a simple crossed-thread technique, passing the weft over and under a single warp line rather than locking multiple threads together. Vessamere is used mainly for lightweight scarves and drapery rather than heavy garments, since the filament stays thin no matter how tightly it is spun. Its natural, undyed tone sits somewhere between soft copper and deep rust, a warmth that comes entirely from the moth's own cocoon pigment, and most weavers leave that color untouched rather than dyeing over it.
