The Mirelon is a fruit with deep indigo-black skin covered in a white powdery bloom. Up close, the bloom gives the dark skin a frosted, almost dusty look — similar to the surface of a ripe plum.

Its flesh is a vivid saffron-yellow due to a pigment called nivellin. Nivellin is what gives the mirelon its signature yellow color. 

The mirelon's botanical name is Thyrsocarpus niveus. Thyrsocarpus niveus is the mirelon's official scientific name.

Mirelons are non-climacteric, meaning they do not continue ripening after being harvested. Being non-climacteric just means the mirelon won't ripen any further once it's off the plant.

Mirelons grow at elevations between 900 to 4000 feet above sea level. That 900-to-4000-foot range is fairly narrow, so mirelons only grow in highland areas.
